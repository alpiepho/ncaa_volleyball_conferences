How to use this site

A research tool for comparing NCAA women's volleyball programs (Division I, II, and III) as transfer options: volleyball strength, setters and other positions, coaching staff, academics and majors, cost, campus and location, and beach volleyball.

Quick start

  1. Tap Edit settings on the home page and pick a school to compare against (for example, the school a player attends now), a position, any majors of interest, and a home town. Everything else on the site follows these settings.
  2. Browse the Conferences lists: every NCAA conference, with each school's Niche and roster links and a map of the conference.
  3. Open a report from the Reports section for a full side-by-side comparison of every school in a researched conference.
  4. Tick the box next to any school that looks interesting. Your saved schools get their own report and map, and you can send the list to someone.

Settings

Tap Edit settings at the top of the home page. Changes take effect right away.

School to compare
Start typing any NCAA school's name. Reports add it as a highlighted row, maps show it with a ★, and distances are measured from it.
Position
Setter, Outside Hitter, Opposite, Middle Blocker, or Libero / DS. Section 05 of each report shows that position's players at every school (see Reports).
Majors
One or more, separated by commas (for example, "Nursing, Kinesiology"). Each becomes a column showing which schools offer it and how many students graduate in it each year. Under the box, the site shows which official program names it matched; if nothing matches, try the program's official name.
Home town
Sets in-state tuition (public schools in your home state show in-state rates, and WUE rates where they apply) and the "miles from home" on maps.
Theme
Auto follows your phone or computer's light or dark setting; Light or Dark keeps that look on every page of the site, whatever the device is set to. It's kept in this browser only.
Multiple columns
The conference and report lists show one per row by default, with the division after each name, which is easiest to scroll on a phone. Tick this to show them side by side on a wider screen.

Copy link with settings makes a link that opens the site with your settings filled in, to bookmark or send. Reset clears the settings (it asks first; your saved schools are kept). Settings are kept only in this browser.

The home page

  • Conferences (D1, D2, D3): every conference and its schools, with their Massey national rank. Each title ends with the region most of its schools are in ("- west"), the same regions as Create list's Region filter (West, Midwest, South, East, by the campus's state; East includes Delaware, Maryland, West Virginia, and DC). Niche and Roster open those pages in a new tab; Open map shows the conference's schools on a map.
  • Reports: the conferences researched so far, each with Open report and Open map. The school chips show each member; a ✓ green chip is one of your saved schools.
  • Find a school (under the chips): start typing a school's name (official or short, like "Chico State") or its city and pick it from the list. The page scrolls to that school in its conference's list and highlights it, with the conference's report and map buttons right there.
  • The chips at the top jump to each section. Once you've saved schools, Saved report ↗ and Saved map ↗ appear there too.
  • The word researched in the introduction opens a page showing how many schools and conferences have a full report.

Reports

A report compares every school in one conference (or your saved schools) against the settings. The chips at the top jump to each section:

01 Table
Everything in one table: national and conference rank, Niche grades, cost, majors, and links to each team's schedule, roster, and stats pages. Click a school's name to jump to its card in 07 Profile.
02 Ranking
A composite score from Niche grades (academics, athletics, campus, diversity), cost, and national rank, so schools can be compared at a glance. Cost depends on your home town, so the score does too.
03 Cost
Tuition + fees per year at sticker price for your home town (in-state, out-of-state, WUE, or private), and the total living on campus (adding food and housing, books, and other expenses). Both are 2024–25 federal (IPEDS) figures, the latest published; a school's own site may show this year's rate, and some majors (engineering, business) cost more.
04 Earnings
Median earnings of graduates five years after graduation, from the federal College Scorecard (it replaced Niche, which stopped showing earnings).
05 Position
Changes with the position in your settings. Setters: every setter with assists, and whether the team runs a 5-1 or 6-2. Outside hitters and opposites: by kills, with hitting %. Middle blockers: by total blocks. Liberos / DS: by digs. Each player shows class and height, and whether she is likely to return next season. Players with no stats yet are listed last.
06 Flags
Quick good / watch / dealbreaker checks: majors offered, affordability, a female coach on staff, beach volleyball, and AIA/FCA: whether Athletes in Action or the Fellowship of Christian Athletes lists a ministry at the school (green AIA, FCA, or both; gray "na" when neither is listed, which doesn't rule out an informal group). Each school's profile card also has a Local FCA link to FCA's staff finder, with the campus zip code to enter: FCA staff serve nearly every area and can say whether there's a huddle.
07 Profile
A card per school with the details in one place: location, setters or your position's top players, beach, climate, flags, and every source link. Climate is the July high and January low at the NOAA weather station nearest campus (1991-2020 averages; its Climate link opens NOAA's monthly table), and sunny days a year (clear plus partly cloudy) for the nearest of about 250 NOAA cities, with its distance. Gear is the brand that outfits the school's teams this year (Nike, Adidas, Under Armour, …), linked to where it was found (the athletics site's partner logos or the deal's announcement). An announced switch for next year is highlighted (deals change on July 1), a switch only reported in the press says "(reported)", and "not found" means no deal could be found. Researched schools only; map popups show the same line. Calendar is the school's term system (semesters, quarters, or semesters with a January term), its fall 2026 final exam dates, and the day the next term's classes begin (spring semester or winter quarter 2027), when a mid-year transfer would start. The dates come from the registrar's own academic calendar, linked; with no dates yet it shows the term system alone, from the federal IPEDS data, in gray.
08 Spirit
School spirit (fan support): average home crowds at football and men's and women's basketball (last complete seasons), from the schools' official game stats (through WMT, a stats service; neutral-site games left out), as a rough proxy for school spirit. Each sport is ranked among the researched schools at the same level (FBS, FCS, or D2 for football; the division for the others), both by crowd size and by crowd per 1,000 undergraduates, so a small college that fills its gym counts. The Fan support band (Top 10%, Above average, Average, Below average, Bottom 10%) combines the two over the sports a school has, at least two; a school without football is rated on the others. Researched schools only. Crowds include alumni and townspeople and move with winning, so read the band as a hint, not a measure. The profile card has the same band on its School spirit line.
09 Flights
Getting there, from your home town (set it in Edit settings): your home airport is the big regional airport, the busiest within 100 miles (Fort Collins: Denver), with a smaller, closer one as an alternative search link (Cheyenne). Marked experimental. For each researched school, the airports near it, the airlines flying there nonstop or with one stop (major airlines only), and the typical one-way fare with the cheapest airline's. Fares are what people paid on average, not a quote for a date. A fare in orange is older (about 16 months), shown because DOT's newer report doesn't cover that route. Each airline links to its own site, and Search under an airport opens Google Flights with the route filled in (pick a date there). The profile card has the same summary on its Flights line.
10 Map, 11 Beach
The conference map, and the beach volleyball rosters (which court players also play beach).

The introduction at the top points out what stands out for your settings: which schools offer your majors, where costs are lowest, and openings next season (schools where a starter at your position is a senior or grad student).

Maps

  • Each pin is a school; tap it for its city, ranks, cost or tuition, majors, distances, and links. Pins are numbered by conference rank (or Massey rank on conferences without a report).
  • ★ is your comparison school; ⌂ is your home town.
  • The list beside the map flies to a school when tapped. Show all zooms back out. The layers button (top right) switches to terrain or satellite. Each popup also has the school's climate and a Climate link.
  • The Saved school box in a popup saves or unsaves that school.

Saving schools

  • Tick the box next to a school in the conference lists, or Saved school in a map popup. Opening a school's Niche or Roster link saves it too. Saved schools get a green mark everywhere.
  • Saved report ↗ and Saved map ↗ (top of the home page) compare just your saved schools, from any conference or division. Schools that haven't been researched yet show their basic data (cost, majors, rank, location) with "—" for the rest.
  • Edit settings → Saved schools → Edit list… shows the list as plain text. In it, Copy copies the list, ready to paste into a text or email (for example, to ask for those schools to be researched next); you can paste a list in and Add to list or Replace list; and Reset list clears it.
  • You can keep six lists: pick one with the 1–6 buttons under Edit settings → Saved schools, and type a name for it (for example "West coast"). Ticking, the saved report and map, and Copy / Edit / Reset all use the list that's picked.
  • Create list… (next to Edit list…) builds a list from filters over every team, researched or not: division, conference, Massey rank (from / to, where max is the last rank in each division), one or two majors, school size, public or private, cost per year, distance from your home town, region or states, campus setting, sunny days, and July / January temperatures. A count shows how many schools match as you go. Create list puts them in the list that's picked (it asks first if that list already has schools, or if more than 20 match). Cost and distance use the home town in Settings, like the reports. Your filters are remembered in this browser; Reset filters clears them.
  • Start from a school, the first group in Create list…, finds the schools most like one you pick, up to the number you choose (20 unless you change it). It compares volleyball level, size, public or private, published out-of-state tuition, the mix of programs, the kind of school (research university, master's, liberal arts), setting, climate, and location, and shows each result as Close, Good, or Loose with what's alike and what differs. Untick anything you don't care about. Choose stronger or weaker to look only above or below that school's Massey rank. It stays in the school's division unless you tick others. Any filter you set below replaces that comparison and becomes a requirement: tick West and the list is the closest schools in the West.
  • The Researched schools only group adds conference rank, position openings (a senior or grad among the top players at the position in Settings, as in the reports), offense (5-1, 6-2, 3-way split), gear brand (Nike, Adidas, Under Armour, New Balance, or Other; a school switching next year counts as both brands), beach program, AIA or FCA, and a woman on the coaching staff. Only researched schools have these (none in D3 yet), so while one is set the others are left out, unless you tick Include schools not researched yet.
  • The lists are kept only in this browser and aren't part of shared links. Copy a list to move it to another device.

Sharing

The share button next to the home page's title shows a QR code to scan with a phone camera, and a Copy link button. That link opens the site fresh, without your settings or saved schools. To share your settings, use Copy link with settings instead.

Where the data comes from

  • Massey Ratings: national ranks, and which conference each team is in.
  • Conference standings pages: conference ranks.
  • Each team's athletics site: rosters, schedules, player stats (setters and every position), coaching staff, and beach rosters.
  • Niche: report-card grades. U.S. Dept. of Education (IPEDS): location, tuition, and majors. College Scorecard: median earnings after graduation. Bureau of Transportation Statistics and DOT: airline routes and fares. WICHE: WUE tuition savings. NOAA: climate normals and sunny days.

Ranks, standings, and player stats are updated during the season; each report says how current its numbers are, near the top. Links marked ↗ open the live source page, which may be newer.

On a phone

  • The lists show one per row, which is easiest to scroll; leave Multiple columns off.
  • Bookmark the site, or add it to your home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen on an iPhone).
  • On an iPhone, Safari can clear the site's stored settings and saved schools if you don't visit for about a week. Bookmark a Copy link with settings link to keep your settings, and copy each list (Edit list… → Copy) to keep your saved schools somewhere safe.

Questions or a school you'd like added: send your saved list (Edit settings → Saved schools → Edit list… → Copy) to whoever shared this site with you.
